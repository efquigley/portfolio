## Projects

### Ideological Scaling with Presidential Executive Orders

As a graduate research assistant I worked on a team to create an ideological scale of Presidential Executive Orders concerning race. I preprocessed over 700 texts using R including packages quanteda, dplyr, and stringr. Then using packages distrom and textir, I fit a multiple inverse regression model to analyze which terms used in executive orders were most predictive of certain political ideologies. Using ggplot2 I visualized this data, creating an ideological scale of the past four presidents to ensure face validity, and displaying most impactful tokens for each political ideology.

Key words: Executive orders, ideology scale, multiple inverse regression model

### Uncovering Race and Gender bias through an Original Experimental Survey Design

This research concerns the relationship between race and gender, and perceptions of a person in a professional position. Respondents were shown news articles detailing a chairperson’s plan to help prepare a United States community for incoming floods and heatwaves. One of four articles was randomly assigned to respondents, each including either a white chairwoman, a black chairwoman, a white chairman, or a black chairman. Dependent variables—perceived trust, competence, and ability to solve the issue—were tested on a 5-point scale. In my analysis, I found significance in dependent variables when interacting gender of the participant with their assigned article, and when interacting partisanship with the assigned article. Results showed that women were significantly more critical of white women than men were. Also, Republicans perceived black and white women as significantly more incompetent than Democrats, and perceived white women as significantly more untrustworthy and unable than Democrats.

Key words: race, gender, bias, survey design, experimental design
